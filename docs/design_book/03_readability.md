# 03 — Readability: Players Navigate Without a Map

## Light is signage

Players walk toward light. Place light sources at: doorways players should
enter, path forks (brighter = main route), objectives. Never light a dead end
brighter than the route. In dark maps this is the PRIMARY navigation channel
(see 06_horror).

## Zone identity

Each zone gets a distinct combination of (kit + dominant color + light
temperature). A player teleported anywhere should name the zone in 2 seconds.
- Zone A: warm orange / wood kit; Zone B: cold blue / metal kit; etc.
- Transitions between zones get a gateway moment: an arch, a door, a bridge —
  not a gradual mush.

## Landmark visibility from decision points

At every fork or door, at player eye height (~170 cm), at least one
orientation cue must be visible: a landmark, a unique prop, a light, signage.
Verify with screenshots taken AT PLAYER HEIGHT at each fork — god-view
screenshots lie about readability.

## Differentiate the same-looking

Repetitive spaces (corridors, labyrinth) disorient. If disorientation is NOT
the goal: number/color the sectors, vary wall trim, unique prop per junction.
If it IS the goal (horror labyrinth): keep micro-differentiators anyway —
players need subconscious anchors to feel "lost but learning" instead of
"random and cheated": a crack here, a doll there, a distinct sound source.

## Gameplay-relevant must contrast with decor

Interactables, pickups, doors that open: higher contrast, rim light, or
distinct color family. Decor doors that never open should NOT look like
gameplay doors — players hate trying every fake door.

## Text and signs

Use sparingly; iconography and lighting beat words. If signs are needed,
`sign_spawn_bulk` / `text_place` — max ~5 words, high contrast, at eye height.

Sources: The Level Design Book (wayfinding), Epic Level Design Best
Practices; horror-labyrinth considerations from this project's target genre.
